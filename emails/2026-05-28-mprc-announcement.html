Dear all,

Following the success of our first session last month, we are writing to invite you to the second session of the Montreal Psychedelic Research Consortium (MPRC) Monthly Symposia, co-organized by the Bzdok Lab at MILA and the Psychedelics Contemplation Lab at McGill/the Jewish General Hospital, co-directed by Drs Kyle Greenway and Michael Lifshitz.
As a reminder, these talks aim to create a regular space in Montreal for cross-disciplinary discussions of psychedelics among researchers working in the field. We hope to bring together people from clinical research, neuroscience, psychiatry, psychology, computation, philosophy, and related areas. We also hope to foster a stronger local research community and spark new collaborations and research initiatives across Montreal.

Our second session will feature Dr. Rosemary Bagot, who will give a presentation entitled:
Profiling the Cell-Type Specific Effects of Psilocybin in Medial Prefrontal Cortex
The ever-increasing burden of psychiatric disorders and limitations of current treatments have fueled enormous interest in the therapeutic potential of psychedelics. Clinical and pre-clinical findings point to the prefrontal cortex as key locus of psilocybin’s therapeutic effects but how psychedelics, such as psilocybin, produce lasting therapeutic effects is unclear. I will present work in mouse medial prefrontal cortex using electrophysiology and single-cell RNA sequencing investigating mechanisms of sustained effects of psilocybin. Our data point to a previously overlooked deep-layer neuronal population that is strongly regulated following treatment. These effects are associated with plasticity-related transcriptional changes that align with 5-HT2C receptor expression rather than the canonical 5-HT2A receptor. Blocking 5-HT2C receptors prevents psilocybin’s sustained synaptic effects, highlighting a potential mechanism underlying its long-lasting neuroplastic actions.
The session will be held in hybrid format, though we strongly encourage in-person attendance if possible. The plan is to have approximately 30 minutes of presentation, followed by one hour for questions and open discussion (followed by a 5à7 social event nearby for those who are interested !).
If you plan to attend, please fill out this sign-up sheet so we can keep track of participation and plan accordingly.
We would be delighted to have you join us.

Best,
Sara & Pedro

Dr. Rosemary Bagot is an Associate Professor in the Department of Psychology at McGill University and the Canada Research Chair in Behavioural Neurogenomics. She is also a Primary Investigator at the Ludmer Centre for Neuroinformatics and Mental Health,  a member of the Centre for Studies in Behavioral Neurobiology, and an Associate Member of the Department of Psychiatry at McGill University.
